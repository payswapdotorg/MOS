# MOS Architecture Lock — v1.7 Marketing Engineering Lab

Status: FROZEN

1. v1.7 is additive to v1.6; all v1.6 frozen authorities and constraints remain in force.
2. The Lab is a simulation/strategy-engineering layer, not a replacement for Growth Mission, Workflow, Execution, Evidence, Experiment or Learning.
3. Lab Runs are distinct from real business Experiments.
4. Historical replay is evidence-backed; counterfactual outcomes are modeled estimates and must be labeled.
5. The durable content corpus is reference-first by default; media retention is optional and provider/rights gated.
6. Provider-specific media acquisition rules are enforced at the acquisition boundary.
7. The Lab must never infer redistribution rights from a public URL or social connection.
8. Multimodal features and idea abstractions are versioned and retain source lineage.
9. Corpus completeness is never assumed; coverage/saturation and uncertainty are explicit.
10. The platform simulator models observable behavior and must not invent hidden provider internals.
11. User, creator and platform dynamics are stochastic/stateful and reproducible from recorded run configuration and seed.
12. A time-machine lag prevents future information leakage by enforcing an observation cutoff.
13. Counterfactual branches must disclose the world-model version and uncertainty.
14. The first learning stages must support supervised response modeling and off-policy evaluation before requiring online RL.
15. Reward definitions are mission-specific, versioned and business-outcome-first.
16. Simulator ensembles and robustness evaluation are required before a candidate is considered deployment-ready.
17. Agent Bodies are MOS-owned contracts inhabited by models selected through the existing AI runtime; v1.7 creates no second model router.
18. Agent Organizations are graphs of Agent Bodies; organization search may change topology, roles, tools and model assignment.
19. A generalist single-agent baseline must always be available for comparison.
20. Capabilities are explicit contracts with inputs, outputs, constraints, evaluation, cost, latency and provenance.
21. Missing capabilities may be acquired through Arena or other providers behind Integration; no second MOS marketplace authority is introduced.
22. Human capability acquisition is optional and cannot become a prerequisite for ordinary autonomous growth.
23. Real experiments execute only through v1.6 Mission, Policy, Rights, Distribution, Integration, Workflow and Execution authorities.
24. The Lab never publishes directly through provider APIs outside the existing social adapter/distribution boundaries.
25. Real experiment outcomes may calibrate world models but cannot retroactively rewrite historical evidence.
26. Calibration versions are immutable/append-oriented and linked to the observations that produced them.
27. Simulator policies that exploit prohibited behavior are invalid regardless of reward.
28. Fake engagement, inauthentic behavior, anti-abuse bypass, impersonation, deceptive attribution and rights circumvention are forbidden.
29. All Lab state and artifacts are tenant/workspace scoped.
30. Long-running Lab work runs on durable worker infrastructure; Vercel Hobby Cron is not the scheduler authority.
31. CopilotKit, OpenMuse and Code-OSS are not architectural dependencies.
32. A productized social-automation service is an application of the Lab, not a second architecture.


33. v1.7 Amendment CR-007: the Lab searches the full production program, including a first-class no-op/repost option, atomic/composed/discovered transforms, Transform Pawn Agents, human production tasks, Studio format/configuration, organizations, and bottleneck/stopping decisions.

34. Transform Definitions and Transform Graphs are versioned contracts. A discovered transform is not production authority until validated/evaluated and explicitly versioned.

35. Transform Pawn Agents use the existing Agent Body runtime. No second agent runtime or model router is permitted.

36. Human-generated media is an intermediate production artifact. It may be passed into a selected organization for editing, composition and packaging before it becomes a final candidate.

37. Human production dependencies are economic variables. The Lab records expected incremental value, wait, delay cost, acquisition cost, probability, quality impact and alternatives and may wait, substitute, retry, reduce scope or abandon a branch.

38. Content Studio is a MOS-owned AI+Human production runtime that is both standalone and Lab-invoked. It initially supports reaction, audio podcast and video podcast through a pluggable format contract.

39. Studio can load any submitted organization satisfying the Studio compatibility contract, whether Lab-discovered or user-supplied. Organization selection is versioned and explicit.

40. One-person podcasts may use voice, text, avatar, prerecorded, generated or hybrid interviewer representations. The system preserves generated/human provenance.

41. Multi-person podcast sessions may span multiple authorized MOS accounts and devices while preserving each participant's identity, consent, credentials and contribution provenance.

42. Studio outputs are immutable/versioned artifact packages. The Lab can accept, reject, request treatment, request human action, switch organization/transform or abandon. Each treatment creates a new linked output version.

43. The Studio is not a marketing objective, publishing, rights, policy, experiment, evidence, workflow, execution, model-routing or marketplace authority.

44. The Lab-to-Studio contract carries the selected strategy, transform graph, organization version, Studio format, human tasks, acceptance criteria, budget and delay/stopping policy. Studio returns auditable production artifacts, costs, delays, failures and provenance.

45. Any implementation that assumes every successful strategy needs a transform, every production requires human participation, or every human bottleneck must be waited on violates the v1.7 freeze.
